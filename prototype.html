See it before it's built

Apple publishes a dimensional drawing of every device so accessory makers can build a case that fits. The lab does the same for your idea: one sheet that measures the architecture of the app, with no content in it, so you can check the shape before anyone writes code. Then the same drawing moves through the build phases, and you choose how it looks and feels.

How to read the sheet

Codes, not numbers

Every measurement is a variable. S is spacing, H is height, R is radius, T is type, M is motion. The tables on the right give each code's value, and derived values show their formula, so a change to one number ripples through the whole sheet.

Slots, not content

Dashed boxes are slots: a title slot, a content slot, an image slot. HUG means the box is sized by what goes in it, FLEX means it takes the room that is left, FILL means it spans its parent. Your real words and images arrive later.

Layers and details

The exploded view lifts the page into layers, from the viewport up to the controls. Detail circles on the plan point at features drawn larger: the controls with their 44 px touch area, the nested corner, the radii, the type scale, and the motion timing.

What AIG does with it

Nothing here is a promise of a finished product. The sheet is a conversation piece: bring it, mark it up, and the lab builds the prototype from the agreed shape. Most builds still take a week or more, and AIG may not be able to meet every request.

AI disclosure

This site was coded with Anthropic's Claude under Marc Watkins's direction. Marc supplied the vision and the language about prototypes, timelines, sharing, and the reflection. The build form offers optional AI assistance: only if you choose it, Jev, a TypeSafe AI model, reads your idea and suggests a pathway, and its suggestions are labeled as AI-generated. The preview on this page is drawn by plain code from your choices; no AI model is involved.

The look of this site is Understory: its colours, marks, and drawings of the Grove are generated in code. The drawings are stylised, not traced from photographs. The Grove on the home page is painted leaf by leaf, an homage to the painting method of Theora Hamblett (1895–1977) of Oxford, Mississippi; no image of her work is used, and this site is not connected with her estate or the University Museum.

Accessibility

This site aims for WCAG 2.1 AA: keyboard navigation, visible focus, labeled fields, sufficient contrast, and reduced motion when your system asks for it. The drawing has a text description, and the prototype's buttons are real buttons. If something doesn't work for you, email mwatkins@olemiss.edu.
